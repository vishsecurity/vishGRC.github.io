Audit management systems · ISO 19011 guidance

Audit program & workbench

Plan risk-based audits, assess any chosen compliance criteria, document evidence, and follow findings to closure.

Understand the standard: ISO 19011 provides guidance for auditing management systems; it is not itself a conformity requirements checklist or a certification guarantee. This workspace stores audit plans and evidence records. Choose the actual audit criteria separately (for example ISO 42001, ISO 27001, NIST AI RMF, a regulation, or internal policy), confirm them with your organization, and consult the current licensed standard. Prototype records are not production evidence.

Auditing principles
Use these as professional conduct and quality prompts; they do not replace the licensed guidance.
IntegrityAct ethically; disclose conflicts and material limitations.
Fair presentationReport evidence, criteria, scope limitations, and conclusions accurately.
Due professional careUse sound judgment, appropriate skill, and proportionate diligence.
ConfidentialityProtect audit information and use it only for authorized purposes.
IndependenceManage impartiality and conflicts appropriate to the audit context.
Evidence-based approachBase findings on verifiable, relevant evidence and documented sampling.
Risk-based approachPrioritize audit focus, timing, and effort by objectives and risk.
Auditor competenceAssign a capable team; consider technical, sector, and audit skills.
Audit lifecycle
Apply to first-party, supplier, or external audits; tailor independence and governance to the engagement.
1 · InitiateAuthority, objectives, scope, criteria, feasibility, confidentiality.

Product guide

GovernanceGuard Help Center

What this app is, how to use it, and where its pieces live.

Governance · Risk · Compliance

A practical workspace for connecting risks, controls, audits, and evidence.

GovernanceGuard is a local-first GRC workbench. It helps a team keep key program records together, follow work from planning through reporting, and see a current snapshot of what needs attention.

◎

Why it exists

The idea is to make everyday GRC work easier to follow by bringing registers, audit workpapers, owners, deadlines, and evidence into one connected workspace instead of scattered notes and spreadsheets.

◇

How it is designed

It pairs a browser dashboard with an API and a local SQLite database. That keeps the prototype straightforward to run and lets saved records remain available after a restart on the same machine.

✦

Who made it

GovernanceGuard is made by Vishal Chaudhary. It is a practical, evolving software project—not a certification product or a substitute for professional judgment.

What you can do

Use the navigation to move between working areas. Saved registers and audit records are stored in SQLite; board and overview figures are generated from those records.

!

Manage risk & controls

Create and maintain risk assessments, owners, review dates, control mappings, test dates, and effectiveness status.

✓

Plan and perform audits

Set audit objectives, scope, criteria, team details, and dates; document test procedures and samples; record results and findings; and follow corrective actions.

▣

Keep evidence with work

Attach supported files to audit tests, then download or remove them from the workpaper. Files are kept locally outside the web root.

↗

Track obligations

Record regulatory or internal obligations, applicability, owners, due dates, and implementation status for your own review.

✳

Oversee AI systems

Maintain an AI system inventory and AI risk records, including purpose, provider, accountable owner, impact, lifecycle, and oversight notes.

▤

Report and administer

Review current portfolio snapshots and export a CSV board pack. Admins can configure the workspace and create accounts with Admin, Editor, or Viewer access.

Hosting on your laptop or in the cloud

For everyday use, keep GovernanceGuard on your laptop. Cloud hosting is possible on an Ubuntu VM with persistent storage, but the app is still a prototype and should not hold sensitive evidence or be exposed publicly without a security review.

⌂

Run on this laptop

Install Node.js 20+, run npm install once and npm start, then open http://127.0.0.1:3000. Keep the terminal running; Ctrl+C stops the app. Your SQLite database and evidence remain in the local data/ folder.

↗

Share on a private LAN

First create the Admin account on the laptop itself. Then start with HOST=0.0.0.0 PORT=3000 npm start, allow port 3000 only on your trusted private network, and use the laptop's private IP from the other device. This is plain HTTP; do not use public Wi-Fi or upload sensitive files.

☁

Deploy to a cloud VM

Use an Ubuntu VM with persistent disk (such as Oracle Cloud Always Free if available, or another VPS), not a static/ephemeral host. Install Node.js 20+, run the app as a systemd service with database and evidence paths on persistent storage, and keep it bound to 127.0.0.1.

🔒

Add HTTPS with Caddy

Point a domain's DNS record to the VM, allow ports 80/443, and configure Caddy to reverse-proxy the domain to 127.0.0.1:3000. Do not expose port 3000 publicly. Use a strong unique bootstrap admin password and protect the environment file.

Before cloud use: back up both SQLite and uploaded evidence, patch the server regularly, and get a security/privacy review. HTTPS does not make this prototype production-ready. Full Ubuntu VM and Caddy commands are in README.md under “Hosting on your laptop or in the cloud.”

Important: understand the limits

This is a local prototype, not production-ready compliance or security software. Seed data is illustrative; verify or replace it. The regulatory tracker is not an authoritative legal source, and reports are current snapshots—not historical trends. ISO 19011 provides audit guidance; it is not itself audit criteria or proof of conformity.

Evidence is stored locally and is not malware-scanned, encrypted at rest, or automatically backed up. The prototype also lacks features such as MFA and a tamper-proof audit trail. Do not expose it to an untrusted network or upload sensitive evidence without first adding appropriate safeguards, backups, and a security/privacy review.

For installation and configuration, see README.md. Run the automated checks with npm test.
